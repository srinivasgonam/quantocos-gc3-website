    The month, drawn

    Automation Pod, as a month of credits

    In short

    The Automation Pod is a custom-scoped Quantocos engagement that removes manual work with AI. It begins with a per-department automation audit, then builds simple, standard or complex workflows, retrieval chatbots on your own content, AI voice agents and custom agents, scoped and quoted on request.

    What it replaces

    The hire or the supplier this stands in for

    A growing operations headcount, and the internal automation project that never gets prioritised against delivery work.

    Enterprises do not stall on AI because the models are weak. They stall on procurement: every capability arrives with its own license, its own integration and its own specialist, and none of the three can be scaled across the business. Most departments cannot even describe which of their processes are automatable.

    The credit maths

    How a scoped engagement is estimated

    Scoped first, then billed at the same rate as everything else. A two-week discovery for one department is typically thirty to forty credits, a simple two-system workflow around twenty, and a complex workflow with a human approval step around sixty.

    Buying questions

    What to settle before you subscribe

    The Automation Pod is a scoped Quantocos engagement that finds and removes manual work. It starts with a per-department audit, then builds workflows, chatbots, voice agents and role-specific AI agents, scoped on the same terms as every other pod.

    The audit is scoped first, because automation depth varies too much to publish an honest fixed price. Once scoped, the work is quoted on the same terms — a credit per unit of work — exactly like the subscription pods.

    Simple workflows move data between two systems. Standard workflows add conditional logic and more than two systems. Complex workflows involve human approval steps, unstructured documents or multiple agents coordinating — and those are where most of the value sits.

    No. Deployments keep a human approval step on anything consequential, and agent activity is monitored and evaluated. The design assumption is that an agent drafts and repeats while a named person approves.

    No. The pod integrates with the systems already in place. Where a client wants the single-license model instead, the platform products replace that surface over time.

    By volume multiplied by monotony: the processes that consume the most hours for the least judgment. The audit ranks them and the pod builds in that order.
