    Who we are

    We started with the delivery model, not the deck

    The company is new. The model is not speculative: a pod of human specialists with agents handling the repeatable work, priced as a monthly subscription with a visible credit pool, is the shape we are selling from day one.

    What we are building on top of it — one environment where generation, editing, planning, automation, marketing and research all live, joined to our own cloud — exists because the alternative is the thing every enterprise is stuck on: forty tools, forty licenses and nobody able to say what the AI strategy actually is.

    Structure

    Five divisions, pods as the unit of delivery, one council

    Pods are the thing you buy. Divisions are where the specialists come from. The Operational Excellence Council sets the standards both answer to.

    01Strategy Consulting division: brand and go-to-market strategy, positioning, research, pricing and operating model design.→
    02Digital Digital transformation and performance: search, answer engines, paid media, analytics and lifecycle.→
    03Creative & interactive Brand, design, content and motion, plus the immersive work — 3D, XR and interactive builds.→
    04Learning Professional learning design and enablement: curriculum, assessment and recognition for client teams.→
    05Dimension — AI & automation The AI operations layer: agent design, workflow engineering and the governance that keeps deployments auditable.→

    Guilds

    Every person belongs to a guild, and the guild owns the standard

    A pod ships; a guild decides what good looks like. Creative, Digital, Strategy, Interactive, Dimension and Force — each with a guild leader accountable for the development of the people in it, and for the quality of what leaves it.

    This is the mechanism that stops a small team from becoming inconsistent. Guild leaders run skill development, competition between pods and the review process, so a client's second month looks like a better version of the first rather than a different company.

    See the six guilds
    In practice

    What a guild actually does to the work

    A guild is not a committee. It is where the standard is set, defended and taught — and where the person who did the work is held to it.

    • Critique sessions on real briefs, not on theory
    • A written standard each guild owns and can be measured against
    • Skill development and progression, assessed rather than assumed
    • One guild per person, aligned to their practice
    How this shapes working here
    Delivery standards

    Who is accountable when something goes wrong

    Every pod has a named manager. Above the pods, the Operational Excellence Council holds the delivery standard, runs quality review, and owns the escalation path a client can use without going through the account team they may be unhappy with.

    In practice that means three things: work is reviewed against a written standard before it reaches a client; a client who is unhappy has a route that does not depend on the manager they are unhappy with; and what one pod learns is propagated to the others rather than rediscovered every time.

    Accountability, in order
    Pod managerOwns the engagement, the calendar and the monthly number
    Guild leaderOwns the craft standard and the development of the people in it
    Operational Excellence CouncilOwns delivery standards, quality review and escalation
    Group partnersOwn the client relationship and the contractual commitment
    Corporate structure

    How the group is structured

    Quantocos Global Systems LLP is the parent entity of GC³ and is responsible for the services delivered through it. It contracts with clients, employs the specialists who deliver the work, and holds the platform products described on this site.

    In some markets, delivery is supported by independent contracted partners. They are not group offices, subsidiaries or agents of Quantocos, they do not speak for the company, and an engagement letter names them explicitly wherever they are used.

    The structure

    An LLP, a set of pods, a guild behind each one.

    Every pod is a named team with a lead, specialists and agents. Guilds sit behind the pods so a specialist is never the only eyes on a piece of work.

    • Pod lead: accountable for delivery and the client relationship
    • Guild: the craft group that reviews work across pods
    • Agents: research, drafts, variants and repeatable production
    • LLP: the contracting entity, so terms and liability are explicit
    Responsible business

    Commitments we can actually report against

    A company incorporated this year has no track record to disclose. What it can do is state the commitments precisely, and publish the numbers as they become real.

    AI assurance

    Human sign-off on every deliverable

    No client output is released by an agent alone. Agents draft and repeat; a named specialist reviews and approves, and the approval is recorded.

    Data

    Client data stays client data

    Client material is not used to train shared models. Tenancy and access are controlled at the tenant level, with role-based permissions.

    People

    Development is someone's job

    Guild leaders are accountable for the growth of the people in their guild, and that accountability is measured rather than aspirational.

    Honesty

    Products in development are labelled

    Every product page states its build status. We do not publish roadmap dates as commitments, and we do not dress up a prototype as a platform.

    Partners

    Delivery partners are named as partners

    Contracted delivery partners are independent entities, not group offices. We say so in the footer, in the register and on this page.

    Reporting

    Figures published when they exist

    Pay-gap, environmental and program reporting follows the first full financial year. Until then, this section states commitments, not achievements.
