    In short

    Onboarding runs in three phases. Days 1-30 cover access, brand ingestion and the first brief. Days 31-60 run the first full delivery cycle through the review gate. Days 61-90 settle into a standing rhythm and end with a month report, at which point you decide whether to continue. The first output is delivered five working days after integration.

    Week one

    Four things we need, and nothing else

    Onboarding stalls when a client is asked for everything at once. We ask for four things, and the first brief can be a single paragraph.

    If any of the four is missing, the manager names what is blocked and what it holds up, rather than starting work that will be redone.

    The list
    • Brand assets: logo, fonts, templates and any tone guidance that exists
    • Access to the tools you already work in: the drive, the board, the channels
    • The name of the person who approves output
    • A first brief, however rough
    The review gate

    Nothing is released until a person signs it

    Agents draft and repeat. A named specialist reviews and approves before anything reaches you, and the approval is recorded against the brief.

    Draft

    The agent produces

    Research, synthesis, resizing, transcription and first-pass generation are handled in the pod, against the brief and the credit estimate.

    Review

    The specialist judges

    A human specialist checks craft, accuracy and brand fit, and either approves or sends it back. Commercial judgment does not sit with an agent.

    Record

    The approval is logged

    Who approved it, when, and against which brief is recorded in the portal, so the review gate is auditable rather than asserted.

    Your call

    You approve the release

    The weekly checkpoint is where you accept, reject or redirect. The pod owns production and the calendar; you own the decisions.

    What we do not do

    Onboarding starts with the work

    Agency onboarding often begins with six weeks of workshops before anything is produced. That is a billing model, not a method.

    • No six-week discovery phase before the first output
    • No strategy deck standing in for work
    • No account manager between you and the pod
    • No minimum term beyond the current month
    • No onboarding fee
    Questions

    Onboarding, answered

    What happens on day one?

    A kick-off call with your named manager. The first-quarter calendar is agreed in that call, access is arranged the same week, and the first brief is opened with a credit estimate before any work starts. There is no discovery phase to sit through first.

    How quickly is the first output delivered?

    Five working days after integration. Integration means access to your tools and the brand material the pod works from. The calendar is agreed before that clock starts, so the first output is not a surprise.

    Who do I deal with during onboarding?

    One named manager, the same person for the life of the engagement. They own the calendar, the brief and the review gate. There is no account team between you and the pod, and no hand-off to a delivery layer once the contract is signed.

    What do you need from us to start?

    Four things: the brand assets, access to the tools you already work in, the name of the person who approves output, and a first brief. The brief can be rough. A list of what is slipping is enough to open one.

    What if the first month does not work?

    You stop at the month boundary. There is no lock-in, no notice period beyond the month, and no termination fee. The credit ledger for the month is yours, which also means you can see exactly what the money bought.

    Next step

    Ninety days from now you will either have the work or the evidence that you should stop.

    Both outcomes are useful. The only bad outcome is a year of retainer and no honest answer.
